Travel and Pittsburgh

Duquesne University sits on the Bluff above the Monongahela River, a ten-minute walk from downtown Pittsburgh and its Cultural District.

Getting here

ByDetails
AirPittsburgh International Airport (PIT) is about 20 miles west of campus, 30 to 40 minutes by car or rideshare. The 28X Airport Flyer bus runs from the terminal to downtown and Oakland.
TrainAmtrak serves Pittsburgh Union Station downtown, on the Pennsylvanian (New York, Philadelphia, Harrisburg) and Capitol Limited (Washington, Chicago) routes.
CarCampus is at 600 Forbes Avenue, Pittsburgh, PA 15282, just off the Boulevard of the Allies and I-376. Parking information will be posted with the final program.

Exploring Pittsburgh

Pittsburgh is a compact city of rivers, bridges, hillside neighborhoods, and museums, and most of what follows is within a short walk, bus ride, or rideshare of campus. Late July is warm, with long evenings.

Rivers and views

  • Duquesne Incline and Monongahela Incline: cable cars up Mount Washington for the classic view of the Point and the skyline, best at sunset.
  • Point State Park, where the Allegheny and Monongahela meet to form the Ohio.
  • Three Rivers Heritage Trail: riverside walking and cycling along the North Shore and South Side.
  • Kayak rentals on the North Shore and bike share stations across the city.

Museums

  • The Andy Warhol Museum, on the North Shore.
  • Carnegie Museums of Art and Natural History in Oakland, with the dinosaur halls and the Hall of Minerals and Gems.
  • Mattress Factory: installation art in the Mexican War Streets.
  • Phipps Conservatory: Victorian glasshouse and gardens at the edge of Schenley Park.
  • Heinz History Center, The Frick Pittsburgh, National Aviary, Carnegie Science Center.

Neighborhoods

  • Strip District: produce markets, Italian groceries, and coffee roasters, walking distance from campus.
  • Oakland: the university district, with the Cathedral of Learning and its Nationality Rooms, Schenley Plaza, and the Carnegie Mellon campus.
  • Lawrenceville: restaurants, bars, and shops along Butler Street.
  • South Side and Mount Washington: East Carson Street below, Grandview Avenue overlooks above.
  • Squirrel Hill and Shadyside: residential streets with good dinner options.

Food and evenings

  • Pittsburgh classics: a Primanti Brothers sandwich with the fries inside, pierogies, and a fish sandwich.
  • Downtown Cultural District: theaters and galleries a short walk from campus, with Market Square in the middle.
  • PNC Park: the Pirates' riverside ballpark. Check the schedule for home games during the conference week.
  • Kennywood: a century-old amusement park with wooden roller coasters, a short drive east.
